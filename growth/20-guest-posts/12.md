# The Vendor Evaluation Checklist: 40 Questions Before You Sign

The demo answers the questions the vendor wants to be asked. This checklist is the ones they'd rather you didn't. Forty questions across six categories — functional fit, technical fit, security and compliance, support, pricing and contract, and migration and exit. You won't need all forty for any given deal, and you can't know which twenty matter until you've asked them all. But every question here has a story behind it: a team that signed without asking, and found out in year two.

## Functional Fit (Questions 1–8)

Functional fit is the category buyers handle worst, because the demo makes everything look possible. The questions below force specificity: each one asks the vendor to commit to what the product does with your data, your workflows, and your team — not what the product can do in general. Answer these in a working session with the people who will actually use the tool, not in a follow-up email with the sales rep, who has never run your workflows.

1. Which of your three most-used workflows does the product replace end to end — and which does it only partially cover?
2. What is the maximum number of users, records, or automations before performance measurably degrades?
3. Which features on the pricing page are actually included at the tier you're buying, versus listed at a higher tier?
4. What will this tool do that your current setup doesn't — expressed as a workflow change, not a feature name?
5. What do the vendor's own customers most commonly say is missing? (Ask the sales rep directly; the good ones will tell you.)
6. Can the vendor name one workflow they explicitly don't support?
7. What are the reporting defaults, and what is the actual limit on customizing them — fields, time ranges, export options?
8. How do roles and permissions map to your real team structure — or will you be forced to adopt theirs?

## Technical Fit (Questions 9–15)

9. What is the API rate limit, and does it apply per key, per account, or per plan?
10. What does the API expose — all records and metadata, or a curated subset?
11. Which of your existing tools have native integrations, and which require middleware you'll have to maintain?
12. What is the uptime SLA, and what does the penalty actually pay — credit, or a discount on a future invoice?
13. Where is your data hosted, can you choose the region, and is residency changeable after signing?
14. How does the product behave at your data volume — have you loaded a realistic dataset in the trial, or only demo data?
15. What does the mobile app lack compared to the web version? (The answer is almost always "something.")

## Security, Compliance, and Support (Questions 16–27)

16. Which certifications do they hold (SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR), and when was the last audit completed?
17. Can they share a penetration test summary, and how recent is it?
18. What is their breach notification process and the contractual timeline to notify you?
19. Who are their subprocessors, where is the list published, and can you be notified when it changes?
20. What does the data processing agreement cover, and what happens to your data at termination — deleted, retained, or archived for how long?
21. Is SSO and SCIM included at your tier, or is it a line item the sales rep forgot to mention until you asked?
22. What is the actual response time per tier — not the brochure SLA, but the median of the last month?
23. Do you get a named contact, or a ticket queue where the rep changes every time?
24. What does "24/7 support" mean in practice — triage around the clock, or resolution around the clock?
25. Where is support located, and does your business hours overlap with theirs by more than a few hours?
26. How are bugs escalated, and how long did the last five critical bugs take to fix?
27. Is onboarding included in the subscription, or is it a billable professional services engagement?

## Pricing and Contract (Questions 28–34)

28. What is the renewal increase clause, in writing, with the percentage cap stated?
29. What is the seat minimum, and does it apply per module — a 25-seat minimum across four modules is really 100 seats?
30. Which usage meters exist beyond seats: API calls, storage, automation runs, AI credits?
31. What is the overage price per unit, not the committed rate — the committed rate is what gets you in, the overage is what gets billed?
32. Can you downgrade mid-term without penalty, or is downgrade only possible at renewal?
33. What is the termination notice period, and is there an early-exit fee or a full-year commitment inside the annual price?
34. Will they lock the renewal rate for a multi-year term, or is every year renegotiated against the new list price?

## Migration and Exit (Questions 35–40)

35. What formats does the data export support — CSV, JSON, API, or a full backup file?
36. What exactly is included in an export: records, metadata, comments, attachments, history, and timestamps?
37. What does the vendor's migration service cost, and who owns the field mapping — you, them, or a partner?
38. Is there an official migration runbook, a documented import wizard, or a vetted partner list?
39. What happens to your data at termination, and how long do you have to retrieve it before it's deleted?
40. What is the realistic total migration cost — vendor hours plus your internal hours — for your actual data volume?

## How to Ask These Questions Without Getting Dismissed

Send the full list in writing and ask for written answers. Written answers become contractual expectations; verbal answers become marketing recollections. Time the responses: a vendor who answers all forty in 48 hours with specifics is showing you how the post-sales relationship will feel, and a vendor who deflects ten questions with "that depends on your implementation" is showing you something too. Watch the pattern across the whole list, not the individual answers — one evasive answer is a phrasing issue; six evasive answers in security, pricing, and exit are a business model.

When you're weighing the answers, treat "value" as the score on your budget, not the vendor's pricing page. That is exactly why independent review platforms publish their scoring rubrics rather than keeping them internal — [PilotStack's methodology](https://www.pilotstack.online/methodology), for instance, documents a five-dimension scoring framework applied consistently across 150+ tools, so a "value" score means the same thing from one review to the next. Your forty-question list is the same idea applied to your own situation: consistent questions, comparable answers, and a decision you can defend.

## Conclusion

Forty questions feels like overkill until the first one saves you from a two-year mistake. Send the list before the demo, not after — it filters out the vendors who can't answer, and it sharpens the ones who can. Then triage: for any given deal, eight to ten questions will carry the real weight, and you'll only know which ones those are after you've asked all forty. The cost of asking is one email. The cost of not asking is whatever your team discovers in year two, when the discount has expired and the exit door is closed.
